# ASD-STE100 boundary

Read this file when the user requests ASD-STE100, when a contract names it, or when the text is safety-critical.

## What the source establishes

ASD-STE100 Issue 9, dated January 15, 2025, is an international standard for technical documentation. It has 53 writing rules in nine sections and a controlled dictionary with 875 approved general words. It also permits subject-specific technical nouns and technical verbs under its rules.

The relevant disciplines include consistent terminology, controlled word meanings, direct verbs, active voice, short sentences, and no phrasal verbs. Procedures use one instruction in each sentence. Descriptions add information gradually and keep one topic in each paragraph. Semicolons are not permitted. Issue 9 caps procedural sentences at 20 words and descriptive sentences at 25 words. Its word-count rules include special treatment for quoted text, identifiers, titles, proper nouns, and other immutable material.

The official standard, not this summary, is the authority:

- [ASD-STE100 official site](https://www.asd-ste100.org/)
- [ASD-STE100 Issue 9 PDF](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
- [Official FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html)
- [Official guidance about checking tools](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STEsoftware.html)

## Claims you may make

Use these labels precisely:

- **Controlled technical prose** — the default branch in this skill. It applies a compact, checkable writing system without dictionary lockdown.
- **STE-inspired** — the draft follows selected ASD-STE100 principles but was not checked against the full official standard and terminology source.
- **ASD-STE100-compliant** — use only when a qualified reviewer checked the text against the applicable issue of the official standard and the approved project or company terminology.

Do not describe the bundled checker, an AI review, or a zero-warning result as certification. The official guidance says tools support writers but do not replace the standard, technical expertise, linguistic expertise, or human judgment.

## Strict source-backed branch

1. Obtain the contractually applicable issue of ASD-STE100 and the approved company, industry, or project terminology source.
2. Identify each immutable string, proper noun, identifier, title, technical noun, and technical verb before editing.
3. Classify each block as procedural, descriptive, or safety text and apply the applicable rules from the official standard.
4. Check every general word for its approved meaning, part of speech, and form in the official dictionary. Do not replace an approved technical term with a simpler but incorrect term.
5. Apply the official word-count method rather than a generic token count when a sentence approaches its limit.
6. Run the bundled checker as an aid, then perform the non-mechanical review: topic progression, terminology identity, meaning, grammatical correctness, technical correctness, and completeness.
7. Route the final text to the required qualified reviewer. Keep the result labeled `STE-inspired` until that review is complete.

For warnings and cautions, use the risk categories and required wording from the governing domain policy. ASD-STE100 controls language. It does not replace the risk analysis or authorize a risk classification.
