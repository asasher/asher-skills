# Captions and export

Create cues from the final edit. Preserve spoken meaning, negation, names, and technical terms; captions are not a paraphrased script. Check uncertain words against the audio or supporting on-screen evidence. Use meaningful phrase boundaries and reading time rather than a fixed number of words per caption.

Follow project typography. For portrait output, normally use one or two sentence-case lines on a contrasting plate over variable footage. Include the plate's padding in collision checks. Keep placement stable within a shot; choose another planned lane at a scene change when needed. Verify the longest cue and every two-line cue at normal playback size.

Keep a clean master and portable timed sidecar (SRT or VTT). Burn captions for the requested delivery variant; the project may prefer burned-in Shorts and sidecars for landscape. Not every uploader accepts sidecars. Check its supported caption path, and avoid unintentionally duplicating burned-in speech with native automatic subtitles while preserving an accessible option.

A useful handoff contains:

- Encoded video, with requested captioned/clean variants clearly named.
- Corrected transcript and timed captions.
- Source-to-output edit list, synchronization choices, and render command/script.
- Editable motion scenes, scene cue timing, fonts, renderer versions, and assets.
- Audio treatment settings and a dry/processed comparison excerpt when voice is under review.
- Validation notes distinguishing decode/measurement checks, sampled visuals, continuous playback, listening, and destination-app checks.

Use a common compatible export such as H.264/yuv420p with AAC and fast-start MP4 unless the brief needs another codec. Preserve intentional aspect ratio and frame rate; avoid unintended stretching, variable timing at joins, duplicate audio tracks, and silent default streams. Decode the complete file and inspect the encoded output at its actual delivery size. Check caption timing after every timeline revision.
